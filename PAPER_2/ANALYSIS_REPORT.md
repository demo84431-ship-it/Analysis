## Executive Summary

This report analyzes **3,650 questions** from **53 UGC NET Psychology Paper 2** examinations spanning **2004–2025**. The analysis reveals **dramatic shifts** in question distribution, topic emphasis, and exam philosophy across four historically meaningful eras. The most critical finding for June 2026 preparation: **the exam has fundamentally redistributed its focus** away from traditional cognitive psychology (Memory, Perception, Learning) toward applied areas (Psychotherapies, Health Psychology), foundational areas (Indian/Eastern Psychology, Research Methods), and developmental/social domains.

## Historical Era Framework

Based on research into UGC NET's institutional history, I identified **four meaningful eras** that correspond to real structural changes in the exam:

### Era 1: Early UGC (2004–2012) — 900 questions
- **Conducting body:** UGC directly (2004-2009), then CBSE (2010-2012)
- **Format:** 50 MCQs per paper, Paper III was subjective until 2012
- **Syllabus:** Older framework, approximately 7 domains
- **Character:** Heavy emphasis on core cognitive psychology (Memory, Perception, Learning = 27% of paper)

### Era 2: Split Paper CBSE (2013–2017) — 1,150 questions
- **Conducting body:** CBSE
- **Format:** Paper II (50 MCQs) + Paper III (75 MCQs), both objective from 2012
- **Syllabus:** Same older framework, but Paper III brought in applied/advanced topics
- **Character:** Broader coverage due to dual papers; applied psychology began gaining weight

### Era 3: NTA Transition (2018–2019) — 400 questions
- **Conducting body:** NTA (from December 2018)
- **Format:** Single Paper II with 100 MCQs, CBT mode, Paper III discontinued
- **Syllabus:** Revised in June 2019 to current 10-unit structure
- **Character:** Transitional period; format stabilized but syllabus was in flux

### Era 4: Modern NTA (2020–2025) — 1,200 questions
- **Conducting body:** NTA
- **Format:** 100 MCQs, CBT, 200 marks
- **Syllabus:** Current 10-unit revised syllabus fully implemented
- **Character:** This is your exam. More balanced distribution across all 10 units; new areas like Eastern Psychology, Psychotherapies, and Applied Psychology are now major.

## Quantitative Analysis: Unit Distribution

### The Great Redistribution

The single most important finding: **the exam has moved from being dominated by 3-4 units to a more even spread across all 10 units.**

| Unit | Era 1 (2004-12) | Era 2 (2013-17) | Era 3 (2018-19) | Era 4 (2020-25) | Trend |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|

### What This Means

- **Unit 5 collapsed** from dominating (27%) to being just one among many (12%). Memory, Perception, and Learning are still tested but at half the historical rate.
- **Unit 1 exploded** from near-zero (1%) to a major section (8%). Eastern/Indian Psychology is now a real exam area.
- **Unit 9 tripled** from 3.4% to 10.3%. Psychotherapies and Developmental Theories now carry significant weight.
- **Unit 8 (Social Psychology)** went from nearly absent (0.9%) to substantial (7.4%).
- **Unit 2 (Research/Statistics)** is now the LARGEST unit at 13.6%.

## Exam Preparation Strategy for June 2026

### Tier 1: HIGHEST PRIORITY (Expect 45-50 questions combined)

These areas should consume **50-60% of your study time:**

#### 1. Research Methodology & Statistics (Unit 2) — Expect ~14 questions
- **Inferential Statistics** (4-5 questions): Non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon, Chi-square, Kruskal-Wallis), t-tests, ANOVA, Factor Analysis, Multiple Regression, Hypothesis Testing, Type I/II errors
- **Descriptive Statistics** (2-3 questions): Measures of central tendency, dispersion, scales of measurement, correlation coefficients
- **Research Methods** (2-3 questions): Qualitative research methods, experimental design, sampling techniques (especially non-probability sampling), action research
- **Research Paradigms** (1-2 questions): Positivism, constructivism, mixed methods, philosophy of science
- ⚠️ **Passage alert:** Statistics is the #1 area for passage-based questions. Practice interpreting data tables and research scenarios.

#### 2. Psychotherapies & Development (Unit 9) — Expect ~10 questions
- **Psychotherapies** (4-5 questions): Behaviour Therapies (systematic desensitization, flooding, token economy), Motivational Interviewing, Transactional Analysis, CBT, Person-Centered Therapy, Psychoanalytic therapy
- **Developmental Theories** (3-4 questions): Piaget's Cognitive Development (all stages), Erikson's Psychosocial Stages, Kohlberg's Moral Development, Vygotsky's Sociocultural Theory, Bronfenbrenner's Ecological Systems
- **Language Development** (1 question): Theories of language acquisition

#### 3. Personality, Motivation, Emotion & Stress (Unit 7) — Expect ~12 questions
- **Personality** (5-6 questions): Psychoanalytic (Freud's defense mechanisms, psychosexual stages), Rogers' Person-Centered Theory, Trait theories (Big Five, Allport, Cattell), Social-Cognitive Approach (Bandura), Murray's personology
- **Motivation** (3-4 questions): Theories of Motivation (Maslow, Self-Determination Theory, Drive-Reduction Theory, Expectancy-Value), Motivational Interviewing
- **Stress & Coping** (2-3 questions): Hardiness, coping mechanisms, stress models (Lazarus & Folkman)
- **Emotion** (1-2 questions): Theories of emotion (James-Lange, Cannon-Bard, Schachter-Singer, Cognitive Appraisal)

#### 4. Eastern/Indian Psychology & Schools (Unit 1) — Expect ~8-10 questions
- **Eastern Systems** (5-6 questions): Buddhism (Noble Eightfold Path, mindfulness), Yoga Psychology (Patanjali's eight limbs), Vedantic psychology, Sufism, Jain psychology
- **Western Schools** (2-3 questions): Structuralism (Wundt, Titchener), Gestalt Psychology (Wertheimer, Köhler), Functionalism, Behaviorism, Psychoanalysis
- **Philosophy of Science** (1 question): Paradigms, epistemology
- ⚠️ **This is a NEW area.** If you studied from old materials, you may have neglected this entirely. It's now worth 8-10% of the paper.

### Tier 2: HIGH PRIORITY (Expect 30-35 questions combined)

These should consume **25-30% of your study time:**

#### 5. Attention, Perception, Learning & Memory (Unit 5) — Expect ~12 questions
- **Learning** (4 questions): Classical Conditioning (Pavlov), Operant Conditioning (Skinner — reinforcement schedules), Learning Theories, Observational Learning
- **Perception** (3 questions): Depth Perception (cues), Gestalt Principles, Perceptual Constancy, Signal Detection Theory, Psychophysics
- **Memory** (3 questions): Working Memory Model (Baddeley), Types of Memory (episodic/semantic/procedural), Types of Long-Term Memory, Forgetting (interference, decay)
- **Attention** (2 questions): Selective attention models, divided attention

#### 6. Psychological Testing (Unit 3) — Expect ~10 questions
- **Types of Tests** (2-3 questions): Intelligence tests (WAIS, Stanford-Binet, Raven's), Personality tests (MMPI, 16PF, Rorschach, TAT), Aptitude Tests, Attitude Measurement
- **Reliability** (2 questions): Types of reliability, Standard Error of Measurement
- **Validity** (2 questions): Construct Validity (convergent, discriminant), Content, Criterion
- **Test Construction** (1-2 questions): Item Analysis, Factor Analysis, difficulty index, discrimination index
- **Norms & Standardization** (1 question)

#### 7. Biological Basis (Unit 4) — Expect ~10 questions
- **Nervous System** (3-4 questions): Brain structure (lobes, functions), Limbic System, cerebral lateralization
- **Neurons** (2 questions): Neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin, GABA, acetylcholine), action potential
- **Genetics** (2 questions): Chromosomal Anomalies, behavioral genetics, twin studies
- **Endocrine System** (1 question): Hormones and behavior
- **Methods** (1 question): Brain Imaging Techniques (fMRI, PET, EEG)

#### 8. Applied & Health Psychology (Unit 10) — Expect ~8 questions
- **Health Psychology** (3 questions): Psychoneuroimmunology, Chronic Diseases, Health Behavior Theories (Health Belief Model, Transtheoretical Model)
- **Wellbeing & Positive Psychology** (2 questions): Mindfulness, Flow, PERMA model, Character strengths
- **Emerging Areas** (2 questions): Cyberbullying, Psychology and Technology, Environmental Psychology
- **Poverty & Marginalization** (1 question): Psychological impact of poverty, social exclusion

### Tier 3: MODERATE PRIORITY (Expect 15-20 questions combined)

These should consume **15-20% of your study time:**

#### 9. Thinking, Intelligence & Creativity (Unit 6) — Expect ~8 questions
- **Intelligence** (3-4 questions): Theories (Spearman, Thurstone, Gardner, Sternberg, Cattell), Intelligence Tests, Group Differences, Emotional Intelligence
- **Thinking/Problem Solving** (2-3 questions): Metacognition, Cognitive Biases, Reasoning, Judgment and Decision Making
- **Creativity** (1-2 questions): Theories of creativity, divergent thinking

#### 10. Social Psychology (Unit 8) — Expect ~7 questions
- **Social Influence** (2 questions): Compliance Techniques (foot-in-door, door-in-face), Obedience (Milgram)
- **Group Dynamics** (1-2 questions): Social Facilitation, Groupthink, Social Loafing
- **Attitudes** (1 question): Attitude formation, cognitive dissonance
- **Prosocial/Aggression** (1 question): Bystander Effect, Types of Aggression
- **Intergroup Relations** (1 question): Prejudice and Discrimination, Theories of prejudice

### Critical Do's and Don'ts

#### DO:
- ✅ **Master Eastern Psychology** — Buddhism, Yoga, Vedanta, Sufism. This is entirely new and heavily tested.
- ✅ **Know your statistics cold** — Non-parametric tests, Factor Analysis, ANOVA, regression. This is the biggest single unit.
- ✅ **Learn all major psychotherapies** — CBT, Behaviour Therapy, Motivational Interviewing, Transactional Analysis, Person-Centered.
- ✅ **Know Piaget's stages inside-out** — Most consistently tested developmental theory (11 questions in Era 4 alone).
- ✅ **Practice passage-based questions** — Especially statistical scenarios and research methodology passages.
- ✅ **Focus on theories by NAME** — The exam tests specific theorist-theory linkages (e.g., "Who proposed X?")
- ✅ **Study Self-Determination Theory** — Rising steadily in motivation questions.
- ✅ **Know Construct Validity deeply** — 7 questions in recent papers alone.

#### DON'T:
- ❌ **Don't over-invest in Memory** — It dropped from 10% to 2.6% of recent papers. Know the basics (Working Memory, types of LTM) but don't go deep.
- ❌ **Don't spend excessive time on old-style Perception questions** — Perceptual illusions and constancies are less frequent now.
- ❌ **Don't ignore Social Psychology** — It was nearly absent in old exams but now carries 5-7% weight.
- ❌ **Don't rely on pre-2019 study materials alone** — The syllabus changed fundamentally. Unit 1 and Unit 10 barely existed before.
- ❌ **Don't skip Health Psychology** — Psychoneuroimmunology, chronic diseases, and health behavior models are now regular.
- ❌ **Don't memorize without understanding** — Questions are shorter but conceptually precise. Surface memorization won't cut it.
